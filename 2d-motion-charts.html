Kinematics equations

Galileo's definitions

v=ΔsΔt
v=vi + vf2
Δv=vf−vi
a=ΔvΔt

The two big equations

Δs=viΔt+aΔt22
vf2=vi2+2aΔs

Launch components

vix=v cosθ
viy=v sinθ

Range (symmetric only)

R=v2 sin 2θg

Signs

X: forward is +. Y: up is +.
Every chart value except Δt gets a sign: write + on positive values too, like +4.2. Launch speed and angle have no sign.

Asymmetric charts

Y up ends at the apex, so its vf is 0. Y down starts at the apex, so its vi is 0. The two Y times add up to the X time.

Gravity

Earth: g = 10 m/s² in 1-sig-fig problems, 9.8 m/s² in 2-sig-fig problems.
Moon and Mars: g is given in the problem.

Lab notebook — discussion questions

1. In every chart, the X row has the same pattern. What is it, and why does nothing ever change it?

2. A marble rolled off a table and a marble dropped from the same table land at the same time. Which boxes in the chart show you that?

3. For a symmetric flight, how does the time to reach the apex compare to the whole flight time? Where do you see that in the chart?

4. Why does an asymmetric parabola need two Y rows? What do the two rows share, and what is different between them?

5. Why is the best launch angle for an asymmetric parabola not 45°?

6. Describe how the velocity of a projectile changes as it goes up and comes back down. What happens to each component?

7. A catapult on Earth has a maximum range of 300 m. What would its maximum range be on the Moon? Use the range equation to explain.

8. Filling out the chart is an organizing tool, not your work. For one problem, write out the calculation for every box you solved, and box your final answer outside the chart with units.
