Kinematics equations

Galileo's definitions

v=ΔsΔt
v=vi + vf2
Δv=vf−vi
a=ΔvΔt

Ramps

a=g sinθ

The two big equations

Δs=viΔt+aΔt22
vf2=vi2+2aΔs

Signs

Horizontal: forward is +.
Vertical: up is +.
Ramps: downhill is +.
Every value except Δt and θ gets a sign: write + on positive values too, like +4.2.

Gravity on Earth

g = 10 m/s² in 1-sig-fig problems.
g = 9.8 m/s² in 2-sig-fig problems.

Lab notebook — discussion questions

1. What is the difference between Δv and v? Find a problem where one of them is zero and the other isn't.

2. A dropped rock speeds up the whole way down, yet its acceleration in the chart is negative. Explain why that isn't a contradiction.

3. When a ball is thrown straight up and caught at the same height, Δs is zero. What does that make v? What does it say about vf compared to vi?

4. On a ramp, why is the acceleration smaller than g? What would θ = 0° and θ = 90° mean for the object?

5. Which equation did you reach for when the problem never mentioned time? Which one when it never mentioned the final speed? Why those?

6. In a 1-sig-fig problem, fill in the chart two different ways (for example, find v from Δs/Δt and then from the average of the speeds). Do you get the same number? Why might you not?

7. Filling out the chart is an organizing tool, not your work. For one problem, write out the calculation for every box you solved, and box your final answer outside the chart with units.
