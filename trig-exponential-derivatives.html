Lab notebook — discussion questions
  1. When f(x) = sin(x) is increasing most steeply (crossing zero going up), what does f ′(x) look like there? When f(x) is momentarily flat (at a peak or trough), what does f ′(x) look like there?
  2. Why does differentiating sin(kx) produce cos(kx), but differentiating cos(kx) produces negative sin(kx)? Why does the sign only show up on one of the two?
  3. For y = AeBx, the derivative is y′ = ABeBx — the same exponential shape, just rescaled. Why does exponential growth (or decay) have the special property that a function's rate of change is always proportional to the function's own current value?
  4. If B is negative (decay), the curve is getting smaller, but is its slope actually zero anywhere on the graph? What does that tell you about how quickly a decaying quantity ever truly reaches zero?
  5. In the two tables, every "m" value in the first table reappears as a "y" value in the second table. In your own words, what does that reappearance mean about the relationship between a graph and the graph of its derivative?
  6. Doubling B (making the trig wave twice as "squeezed" or the exponential twice as steep) doubles the derivative's coefficient. Why does making a graph's features happen twice as fast, along the x-axis, also make its slopes twice as steep?
