How this sim grades a Fermi answer

There's no single correct answer to a Fermi question, so this sim never claims to know what your answer "should" be. It checks two things it can know without an opinion about the real world.

1. Does your own math check out?

Once your chain's units cancel to the target, you fill in numbers for every "version of 1" — whatever numbers you believe, including ones that only make sense for this exact question. In Hard mode you then compute the answer yourself; the sim quietly multiplies through your own numbers in the background and checks that your typed answer matches what they actually come to. This is exact arithmetic, not opinion — get it wrong and the box turns red no matter how reasonable your assumptions were. In Easy mode the sim does this multiplication for you, so this check doesn't apply — the point there is choosing sensible units and numbers, not the arithmetic.

2. Did you bracket your own answer with a real factor of 2?

Once you have an answer, you state your own lower and upper bound around it. The sim checks each side against your own answer — not against any hidden "correct" value it computed — for whether it's genuinely in the neighborhood of half (for the lower bound) or double (for the upper bound), with some room for rounding to a clean number. One significant figure is plenty for these — "300" is just as good as "327.4" — your assumptions were rough estimates to begin with, so your bounds don't need to be any more precise than that. You're welcome to type more digits if you want, they just aren't required.

What this sim can't tell you: whether 4 slices of pizza a week, or 90 minutes a day scrolling, is actually true for the person in the question. That's the part only you can estimate — the whole point of a Fermi question.

Why don’t my units cancel?

This sim doesn't know a dictionary of units — it only checks whether what you typed in one box exactly matches what you typed in another, once it ignores capitalization, extra spaces, and a trailing "s". That's it. No abbreviations, no synonyms.

The fix is always the same: go back and make every box that's supposed to be the same unit say it exactly the same way. This is a real, deliberate part of the skill — dimensional analysis only works when you're honest about what unit each number is actually in, and typing it out yourself (instead of picking from a list) is what makes that your responsibility instead of the sim's.

Stuck? Read your chain left to right. Whatever unit is on the bottom of one fraction needs to say exactly what's on the top of the next one, until only the amber target unit is left.
Lab notebook — discussion prompts

Why a factor of 2?

  1. Fermi graded his own students within a factor of 2. Why is that a fair standard for a question with no way to look up the real answer, when 5% would be a fair standard for a unit conversion?
  2. Two students answer the same question with different, both-reasonable assumptions and get answers 3× apart. Can they both be "right"?
  3. Pick one assumption from your last problem and double it. Does the final answer double too, or does something more complicated happen?

Versions of 1, again

  1. The reading calls "8h sleep / 1 day" a version of 1, even though 8 ≠ 1. In what sense is it still a "1"? What would make it stop being valid?
  2. This sim used to grade your final answer against its own internal guess, and it made every question about a fixed daily or weekly habit feel unfair or trivial. Why might checking your own consistency instead of your correctness actually be the more honest way to grade a Fermi question?
  3. Try the football-field blades-of-grass question from the reading by hand, using the same process this sim walks you through.
