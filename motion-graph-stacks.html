Lab notebook — discussion questions

1. At the top of a ball's flight, what is its velocity? What is its acceleration? How do the three graphs show both answers at the same moment?

2. Why does the velocity graph jump at a bounce, while the position graph only has a sharp corner?

3. Why does a bounce show up as a spike on the acceleration graph? Which way does the spike point for a floor, a wall on the right, and the spring at the bottom of a ramp? Why?

4. A ball speeds up on the way down, but its acceleration is negative the whole time. Use the graphs to explain why that isn't a contradiction.

5. Look at a flight with no bounce. How does the slope of the velocity graph compare in every part? What does that tell you about the acceleration?

6. How can you tell from the position graph alone where velocity is zero? Where acceleration is negative?

7. For a ramp problem, what changes in the graphs if you measure position from the spring instead of from the top?
