Gravity, friction, tension and “normal” are names of forces, not agents. An agent is a thing: Earth, the floor, the ramp, a rope, the scale, the air.

1. A free body diagram is drawn for an object, never for an agent. Why doesn't the force the box puts on the floor belong on the box's diagram?

2. A hockey puck slides across frictionless ice at a constant speed. What pushes it forward? What does its diagram tell you about why it keeps moving?

3. A ball tossed straight up is stopped for an instant at the top. What forces act on it there? Is it accelerating?

4. You stand on a bathroom scale in an elevator. When does the scale read more than your weight? When does it read less? Does the direction the elevator moves matter, or only how its speed is changing?

5. As a ramp gets steeper, what happens to the normal force on a block resting on it? What happens to the friction?

6. In an Atwood machine, is the tension in the string equal to the weight of either block? How can the same string pull up harder than one block's weight and less than the other's?

7. A sign hangs from two ropes. As the ropes get more horizontal, what happens to the tension in each? Can they ever be perfectly horizontal?
