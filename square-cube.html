Square Cube Law

Scale an object up or down and its length, area, and volume don't grow at the same rate. Length scales by a factor F. Area scales by F2. Volume scales by F3.

Lab notebook — discussion questions
  1. A statue is cast twice as tall as the original, in the same proportions. Its footprint area goes up ×4, but its weight goes up ×8. Why might the bigger statue crack at its base when the original wouldn't?
  2. A rope doesn't get stronger by getting longer — what dimension actually makes a rope stronger, and why does that mean thickness matters more than length?
  3. Explain, using area-to-volume ratio, why a mouse loses body heat much faster than an elephant, and why the mouse has to eat far more relative to its own weight just to stay warm.
  4. In the deer/moose problem, the linear factor is 1.8. Why is the area factor not also 1.8 — and why is it correct to get it by squaring, rather than by measuring a whole new footprint?
  5. The balloon problem gives you a volume change and asks for the new diameter. Why do you have to take a cube root here instead of scaling the diameter directly by the volume factor?
