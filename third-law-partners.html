Lab notebook · the rule and discussion questions

FA = −FB

Agent verb object → object verb agent. Same verb, same size, opposite direction.

1. A proper third-law pair has exactly two nouns and exactly one verb. Why does that rule out the table pushing up on a block and Earth pulling down on the same block, even though those two forces are the same size and point in opposite directions?

2. “For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction.” What do the words action and reaction suggest about timing? When does the partner force actually happen?

3. A 2200 kg truck pushes a stalled 1500 kg car. Which pushes harder: the truck on the car, or the car on the truck?

4. A kid kicks a ball. The ball flies off and the kid barely moves. If the two forces are the same size, why are the results so different?

5. When you walk across a room, what is the agent that pushes you forward? What do you push, and which way?

6. “Earth pulls the goat down” has the partner “the goat pulls Earth up.” If those forces are the same size, why don’t we notice Earth moving toward the goat?

7. Can an object be its own agent? Think of a wagon “pulling itself” down the sidewalk.
