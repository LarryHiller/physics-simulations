Fw = m g  ·  F⊥ = m g cos θ  ·  F∥ = m g sin θ  ·  FF = μF⊥  ·  ΣF = m a

Tension has no formula of its own. It is whatever force the string needs to supply, and you find it from ΣF = 0 or ΣF = m a.

1. g can be written as 9.8 N/kg or as 9.8 m/s². Show that the two units are the same. When does each description make more sense?

2. A ball tossed straight up is stopped for an instant at the top. What is its acceleration there?

3. Why does the normal force on a block get smaller as a ramp gets steeper? What is it on a level floor? On a vertical wall?

4. μsF⊥ is the most static friction can be. If you push a heavy crate gently and it doesn't move, how big is the friction force?

5. Why is μk usually smaller than μs? Think about the “mountain ranges” in Reading 1.2.

6. An elevator cable lowers a car that is slowing down. Is the tension bigger or smaller than the car's weight? Does the direction of motion matter, or only the direction of the acceleration?

7. A box slides to a stop on a floor. Why doesn't its mass change how quickly it slows down?
